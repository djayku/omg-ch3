The Big Idea

Consumer Model

"What's in it for me?"

Leave when needs aren't met.

Covenant Model

"How do I love you well?"

Stay when it costs.

04 · 11
Quote · The Consumer Model

"Throughout history there have always been consumer relationships. Such a relationship lasts only as long as the vendor meets your needs at a cost acceptable to you. If another vendor delivers better services or the same services at a better cost, you have no obligation to stay in a relationship to the original vendor."

Tim Keller · The Meaning of Marriage · p. 71
05 · 11
Quote · Vows as a Future Promise

"Wedding vows are not a declaration of present love but a mutually binding promise of future love. A wedding should not be primarily a celebration of how loving you feel now — that can safely be assumed."

Tim Keller · The Meaning of Marriage · p. 91
06 · 11
Quote · The Gospel Pivot

"'He loved us, not because we were lovely to him, but to make us lovely.' That is why I am going to love my spouse. Speak to your heart like that, and then fulfill the promises you made on your wedding day."

Tim Keller · The Meaning of Marriage
07 · 11
The Application

This is the only love
that can sustain a marriage.

Not "I love you because you're lovely"
but "I love you to make you lovely."

When you receive this kind of love from God, it frees you to give it — imperfectly, daily — to your spouse.

Discuss in your groups · 5–7 min each

Four questions

  1. Where do you see the consumer model showing up — in our culture, or honestly, in our own marriage?
  2. Your vows were a future promise, not a present feeling. Where in your week does that promise feel hardest to keep?
  3. "He loved us to make us lovely." How does receiving this from Christ change how you love your spouse on a hard day?
  4. Pick one practical act of covenant love this week. What is it? Tell your spouse tonight.
